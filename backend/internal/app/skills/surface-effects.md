# Surface effects without libraries

One effect per page, on the thing that deserves it. The pointer drives each recipe when a person is there, a scripted path computed from t inside `renderAt(t)` when the page is recorded. Use a seeded generator, never `Math.random`, and honour reduced motion with a still or a short fade.

## Recipes

- **Liquid glass.** Once, in a small canvas, compute a displacement map from a rounded-rectangle signed distance: red holds the x shift, green the y shift, 0.5 means none, normalised by the largest shift. Strong bending at the rim and none at the centre; an inner shadow and a one-pixel bright rim sell the edge. Over the page's own canvas or WebGL scene, bend the backdrop in a shader with that map. Over HTML, the usual route is an SVG filter (`feImage`, then `feDisplacementMap`, with `color-interpolation-filters="sRGB"` on the filter: in the default linearRGB the neutral 0.5 reads as about 0.22 and the whole backdrop shifts) applied by `backdrop-filter: url(#id)`: AwwO's page preview removes filter elements written in the HTML, so build the filter from the script at load, and it bends only in Chromium. Where it does not apply, fall back to a frosted blur, and say which you saw.
- **Holographic foil card.** The card tilts toward the pointer on the snappy spring, at most 14 degrees, driven by CSS custom properties (pointer x and y, background position, opacity). Layers: the art; a glare (a radial gradient at the pointer, overlay blend); a foil (a repeating rainbow gradient at an angle, moving against the pointer, masked to the art's shapes, color-dodge blend); a sparse sparkle texture. The foil shows only where the mask lets it: a rainbow over the whole card reads as a filter.
- **Liquid chrome on a mark.** Draw the mark into a canvas; derive a smooth height from it (a blurred mask or the distance to its edge); take normals from the height's gradient; reflect a procedural environment of soft horizontal bands (sky, horizon, ground) that drift with time and slow noise; offset red and blue slightly near the edges; add a thin dark contour at the silhouette. Keep every pow base above zero: one invalid pixel spreads through any glow.
- **Rain on glass.** Keep two copies of the background, heavily blurred (the fogged pane) and sharp. Drops merge and leave trails, so simulate them at load with a fixed step and tabulate them: big drops slide in short jerks, merge keeping their area, and shed small droplets. Each frame, draw the drops into a map of refraction offset and thickness; the composite shows the blurred copy everywhere and, inside each drop, the sharp copy shifted and flipped as through a lens, with a dark rim and a small highlight.
- **Digital code rain.** The glyphs sit still in a grid; what falls is brightness. Each column runs a sawtooth of time longer than the column, so several drops share a column at one speed and never collide. Brightness is white at the head and fades over about 20 cells; each cell changes its glyph at its own seeded rate. Bloom first, then map brightness onto a palette ramp from black through deep green to near white.
- **Cube turn.** Slides are the faces of a cube, each a quarter turn further and pushed out by half the slide's width, in a preserve-3d stage with a perspective of about 1,200 px. The cube turns by progress on the move curve; each face darkens with its angle to the viewer; a floor shadow scales and blurs with the turn.
- **A fluid that follows the pointer.** Two float targets take turns holding a velocity field: each step multiplies it by about 0.94 and adds the pointer's velocity with a gaussian falloff of about 0.12 of the screen. The picture is sampled at its uv minus the flow times a strength, with a small colour split growing with the flow. For a recording, rerun the field at a fixed step of 1/60 s along a scripted pointer path from max(0, t − 2 s): at 0.94 a step, those 120 steps leave under a thousandth of anything older, and no frame replays the whole film on the software renderer.
- **Cloth.** Verlet integration: each point moves by its last move times a damping, plus gravity times the step squared; then distance constraints (along and across the weave) are relaxed 8 to 12 times a step. In still air a settled point must not move: a shiver of a few pixels is the solver ringing, which more relaxations may not stop; sweep the links forward then backward on alternate passes. Pinned points never move; a constraint stretched past about twice its rest length tears. Fixed steps of 1/120 s. For a recording, compute the states once at load (or replay from the first) and let `renderAt(t)` read the one at t, so a seek back or a repeat draws the same frame.
- **Pointer trails.** A full-window canvas that ignores the pointer, sized by the device pixel ratio: sparkle dust (particles from the pointer with seeded speed, gravity and a fade under a second), a ribbon (points that follow one another with a lag, drawn as one tapered stroke), or a ring on the snappy spring that stretches along its speed. Cap the particles at a few hundred; when the pointer rests, stop emitting, draw until the last particle has faded and the ring has settled, clear the canvas and stop the loop.
- **Interface accents.** A border beam: a short bright arc of a conic gradient, masked to the border, turning at one steady rate (linear). A shimmer: one diagonal highlight crossing a button every few seconds. A counting number: the glide spring (no overshoot: a count to 2,000 never shows 2,030) or the enter curve to its real target, in tabular figures. A beam between two nodes: a gradient pulse travelling along an SVG path. A marquee: its track duplicated so it loops without a seam, paused on hover. Words appearing: each rises a few pixels out of a small blur on the enter curve, a cascade whose members start 2 frames apart at 30 fps and that ends within 15 frames (MOTION.md); group a longer line's words so it stays inside that.
- **Preset entrances.** A small named set (fade-up, zoom, slide, a bounce for one moment, a head-shake for an error) written with the motion language's durations, curves and springs by name. Exits take two thirds of their entrance; reduced motion swaps travel for a fast fade.

## Taste

- One effect leads; a second only when it answers a different question. Glass over glass, foil on everything or a trail over a work tool is noise.
- An effect never carries information alone: the counting number ends on the real figure; the beam joins two things that are really connected.
- Each effect costs a canvas or a full-screen pass: measure a second's frame times in live play and fall back to a still above 20 ms. A recording draws on AwwO's software renderer, films of up to 20 s in 360 s for the whole render (about 0.5 s a frame at 30 fps): time one frame in the sandbox (the same renderer) and fix the recording's choice from that: under `renderAt(t)` the renderer's clock, not the GPU, sets the frame times.

## Before you hand over

1. Which effect, on what, and why that one.
2. The pointer path and the scripted path from t; a seeded generator; reduced motion handled.
3. Shader pow bases clamped; positions finite; particle and frame budgets stated.
4. The fallback where the effect is not supported.
